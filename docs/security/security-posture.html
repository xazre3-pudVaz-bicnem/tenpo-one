How it's protected

Three walls, not one.

No single check stands alone. Any request has to clear every layer in turn — and the last line lives inside the database itself, where even a stolen login can't reach past it.

01

Permission Gate

Application

Before any action runs, the app confirms the person's role is allowed to do it. A waiter simply cannot open payroll; a store manager cannot touch company-wide settings.

02

Row-Level Security

Database

Every database query is automatically filtered to the user's own company and store. There is no way to ask for another business's data — the database itself refuses to return it.

03

Triggers & Grants

Last line, inside the database

Money records and secrets are guarded by rules baked into the database. Payments can't be deleted, staff can't promote themselves, and PINs and bank details are unreadable to the wrong roles — even under a direct attack.

Note: the on-screen menus also hide what you're not allowed to do — but that's a convenience for staff, not the lock. The real enforcement is layers 01–03, and the last two live in the database where the browser can't reach.

What's protected, in plain terms

Six guarantees, and how each was proven.

One tenant can't see another

Restaurant A can never read Restaurant B's sales, customers or staff — not even by crafting a direct request to the database. It comes back empty, every time.

Verified verify-security.mjs · cross-tenant IDOR

Nobody can promote themselves

A staff member — or even a store manager — cannot make themselves an owner or platform admin. The database blocks the change outright, so a leaked account can't grow its own powers.

Verified verify-security.mjs · C1 / C2

Money records can't be tampered with

Payments, refunds and daily closings can't be quietly deleted or edited after the fact. The financial ledger is append-only — corrections are added, never erased — which keeps the books honest.

Verified verify-security-fortress.mjs · F6

Sensitive details stay locked down

Staff PINs, salary, emergency contacts and bank info (stored as the last four digits only) are readable strictly by the right roles. A manager can't pull a colleague's bank details even by asking the database directly.

Verified verify-security-fortress.mjs · F3 / F4 / F5

Errors never leak internals

When something goes wrong, users see a friendly message and a traceable ID — never a raw system error or personal data. Support can follow the ID; an attacker learns nothing about how the system is built.

Verified vitest · error-codes.test.ts

The public doors are hardened

Public forms are rate-limited against spam and brute force, login links can't be hijacked to redirect elsewhere, exported spreadsheets can't smuggle in malicious formulas, and modern security headers are enforced site-wide.

Verified vitest · redirect + csv-injection

How we proved it

We don't just claim it. We attack it — on every release.

Before each release, an automated suite logs in as real users of each role and deliberately tries to cross the lines they shouldn't: reading another company's data, promoting themselves, deleting a payment. Every attempt must fail. If one succeeds, the release stops.

Nothing hidden

The remaining items, in full.

A trustworthy report shows its gaps. None of these are critical or high; each is either a dashboard setting on our hosting providers or a planned refinement.

Medium Spam rate-limiting spans one server only. Works today; moving to a shared store (Redis) makes it airtight across many servers. Planned
Medium Feature on/off switches are a menu convenience. They hide screens but aren't a security wall — the real boundaries (layers 01–03) always apply. Documented
Low Two-factor login isn't enforced yet. Available through our identity provider; a toggle, not new code. Provider setting
Low Content-security policy allows inline scripts. A known trade-off of the framework; a stricter mode is a future refinement. Refinement
Low Failed-login alerting needs a log feed. The data exists; wiring up automated alerts is a hosting-side setup step. Provider setting
Low Login attempts aren't yet app-rate-limited. Our identity provider throttles brute force; app-side limiting is an added layer for later. Provider setting

Every item here is tracked in the engineering security docs. The two graded medium are operational hardening for scale — not data-exposure risks — and neither stands between the product and a confident launch.
